

## The Conductor

The Conductor is the GM and the director of the action movie. The Conductor controls enemies and the world, states relevant values before rolls, keeps combat moving, and asks: “What is your follow-up action?”

## Scenes

A scene is a continuous conflict, chase, hazard, or meaningful sequence. Exhaust abilities refresh at the start of the next scene unless stated otherwise.


# 3. Attributes, Level, and Luck

## Attributes

Every Protagonist has three Attributes, rated 0–12.

| Attribute | Role                                                                                                                                    |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Body      | Combat: Max HP, Movement, Mitigation, Melee Attacks.<br>Roleplay: Physical feats such as strength, Senses (vision, hearing, perception) |
| Mind      | Combat: Healing Threshold, Dodge, Ranged Attacks<br>Roleplay: Reactions, Problem solving and Intelligence, Charisma                     |
| Soul      | Combat: Manifestations, Augments, Luck<br>Roleplay: Empathy, Memory, Supernatural perception                                            |

## Level

Level is derived from total Attribute points.

> Level = (Body + Mind + Soul) ÷ 3, rounded down.

The maximum Level is 12.

| Total Attribute Points | Level |

| 6–8 | 2 |
| 9–11 | 3 |
| 12–14 | 4 |
| 15–17 | 5 |
| 18–20 | 6 |
| 21–23 | 7 |
| 24–26 | 8 |
| 27–29 | 9 |

## Attribute Point Advancement

The Conductor awards Attribute Points through milestones and level-ups.

Each Attribute Point raises one Attribute by 1. Attributes cannot exceed 12.

Whenever total Attribute points increase by 3, Level increases.

If a Protagonist gains enough Attribute Points to increase multiple Levels at once, they receive all missed archetype/skill/manifestation choices.

Attribute Mastery is unlocked by individual Attribute score, not by character Level.

## Luck Dice

Soul governs luck.

| Soul Score | Luck Dice |
| ---------: | --------: |
|        0–2 |         1 |
|        3–5 |         2 |
|        6–8 |         3 |
|       9–11 |         4 |
|         12 |         5 |

## Regaining Luck Dice

You regain all Luck Dice at the start of each session.

You also regain 1 Luck Die when you earn a RETCON, complete a major objective, or the Conductor awards one.

## Spending Luck Dice

Unless a skill says otherwise:

Spend 1 Luck Die to reroll one die or add one die to an attack roll, defense roll, or skill check.

When adding a die, add one die of the same type as the roll’s primary die.

Normally only 1 Luck Die may be spent per roll. Skills such as More Favors may allow more.

The normal maximum skill check pool is 6d6. A Luck Die may be spent to add a die only if the pool is below 6d6.

A Luck Die may always be spent to reroll a die.

# 4. Advancement and Learning

## Currencies

SAM uses two advancement currencies.

### AP

Attribute Points raise Body, Mind, and Soul. Attributes range from 0 to 12.

### MP

Mnemonic Points purchase skills, Techniques, Enhancements, Aptitudes, and Manifestations.

MP is earned by completing missions and through meaningful campaign play.

## Typical MP Costs

| Purchase | Typical Cost |
|---|---:|
| Common skill | 1 MP |
| Advanced skill | 2 MP |
| Technique | 2–4 MP |
| Enhancement | 2–4 MP |
| Aptitude | 1–3 MP |
| Lesser Manifestation | 2–3 MP |
| Greater Manifestation | 4–5 MP |
| Exhaust Manifestation | 5–6 MP |

## Imprint and Realize

There are two ways to learn an ability.

### Imprint

Pay the listed MP cost. Learn the ability instantly.

### Realize

Fulfill the narrative unlock condition during play. Learn the ability for free.

You may always bypass the narrative condition by paying the MP cost.

## Ability Categories

### Techniques

Combat abilities using Beats and keywords such as Cadence, Swift, Exhaust, Passive, and Tag.

Archetype signature abilities are Techniques.

When a Technique needs an attack baseline, use the ability baseline in Section 14, keyed by final Beat cost.

### Enhancements

Passive upgrades that do not stack unless stated otherwise.

Common examples:

- Health: +5 Max HP
- Vitality: +2 H.T.
- Memory: +1 known Lesser Manifestation

### Aptitudes

Permissions allowing crafting, upgrades, and specialized access.

Examples:

- Weapon Tuning
- Armor Tuning
- Handler Mods
- Fracture Tech
- Biotech

# 5. Skill Checks

Skill checks use d6 dice pools.

## Dice Pools

Use the relevant Attribute or Derived score.

| Score | Dice Pool |
|---:|---:|
| 0–4 | 1d6 |
| 5–8 | 2d6 |
| 9–12 | 3d6 |

A skill may add +1d6 to a specific type of check.

Only one skill bonus applies to a check unless an ability explicitly says otherwise.

Some abilities, Handler functions, or circumstances may also add one die.

The maximum normal skill check pool is 6d6.

Skill dice explode like normal d6s.

## Reading the Dice

Roll the pool and read each d6 separately.

| Die Result | Hits |
|---:|---:|
| 1–3 | 0 Hits |
| 4–5 | 1 Hit |
| 6 | 1 Hit + explode |

## Explosion

If a die shows 6:

1. Count 1 Hit.
2. Reroll that die.
3. If the new result is 4+, gain another Hit.
4. If the new result is another 6, explode again.

## Difficulty

| Hits Required | Difficulty |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Easy |
| 2 | Medium |
| 3 | Hard |
| 4+ | Rare / extreme |

## Versus Checks

Both sides roll. Compare total Hits. Use the general tie rule.

If both sides are Protagonists:

- the defender wins ties;
- if there is no defender, the side with the higher relevant Attribute wins;
- if still tied, both succeed or the Conductor chooses the most dramatic outcome.

## Derived Checks

Some checks use two Attributes.

> Derived score = (Attribute 1 + Attribute 2) ÷ 2, rounded down.

Update Derived scores only when the core Attributes change.

| Derived Check | Attributes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Grit | Body + Mind | stealth, crafting, parkour |
| Aura | Body + Soul | performance, intimidation |
| Will | Mind + Soul | deception, insight, mental resistance |

## Critical Success

If every die in the original rolled pool shows a natural 6 before explosions, the player earns a RETCON.

A RETCON allows the player to change one fictional detail before the end of the session, as long as it is not outrageous.

## Attribute Mastery Skills

Each Attribute has its own Mastery track. Mastery unlocks by Attribute score.

| Attribute Score | Skill | Effect |
|---:|---|---|
| 2 | Intention | Exhaust — Reroll any number of 1s on a check using this Attribute. |
| 6 | Precision | Exhaust — Replace one die with a 5 on a check using this Attribute. |
| 10 | Prowess | Once per session — Turn one die into a natural 6 on a check using this Attribute. This die can explode. |

A Mastery Skill can be used only on a skill check that uses that Attribute.

For Derived checks, you may use a Mastery Skill from either Attribute involved.

Only one Mastery Skill may be declared after rolling.

Exhaust and Prowess limits are tracked separately per Attribute.

## Old Skill Conversion

Skills 2.0 remains the skill catalog.

Attribute Modifier is removed.

Where an old skill references Attribute Modifier, use Physical Tier instead.

| Old Reference | New Value |
|---|---|
| Body Modifier | Body Physical Tier |
| Mind Modifier | Mind Physical Tier |
| Soul Modifier | Soul Physical Tier |
| Double Body Modifier | 2 × Body Physical Tier |
| Double Mind Modifier | 2 × Mind Physical Tier |
| Double Soul Modifier | 2 × Soul Physical Tier |

# 6. Health and Survival

## Max HP

Max HP is:

> Max HP = 10 + 2 × Body + Health Enhancement bonuses.

Minimum Max HP is 10.

The bracket summary is:

| Body Score | Max HP Add | Max HP Before Bonuses |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0–2 | +0–4 | 10–14 |
| 3–5 | +6–10 | 16–20 |
| 6–8 | +12–16 | 22–26 |
| 9–11 | +18–22 | 28–32 |
| 12 | +24 | 34 |

## Healing Threshold

H.T. is:

> H.T. = 3 + Mind + Vitality Enhancement bonuses.

Total healing received per round cannot exceed H.T. unless the healing has Overflow.

H.T. resets at the start of each round.

The bracket summary is:

| Mind Score | H.T. |
|---:|---:|
| 0–2 | 3–5 |
| 3–5 | 6–8 |
| 6–8 | 9–11 |
| 9–11 | 12–14 |
| 12 | 15 |

## Healing Actions

| Action | Cost | Effect |
|---|---:|---|
| Use healing item | 1 Beat | Printed healing, H.T. applies unless Overflow |
| Stabilize ally | 1 Beat | Remove 1 Fading Token |
| Healing Manifestation | Printed | Printed healing, H.T. applies unless Overflow |

## Fading

When HP drops to 0 or below, you enter Fading.

While Fading:

- you have 1 Beat per turn;
- maximum Movement is 3 tiles;
- you cannot Chain;
- you take double damage;
- you gain 1 Fading Token at the start of your turn.

At 3 Fading Tokens, you die permanently.

If your HP rises above 0 or combat ends before you die:

- you exit Fading;
- clear all Fading Tokens.

## Stabilize

An ally in Short Range may spend 1 Beat to stabilize you.

Stabilize removes 1 Fading Token. It does not restore HP.

## Deep Wound

If your HP drops to −Max HP or lower, you gain a Deep Wound.

You survive, but your sense of Self is fractured.

Effect:

> In all future combats, you begin at 1 HP.

Cure:

> Only a Trauma Kit can remove a Deep Wound.

## One-Shot Protection

Damage cannot reduce you below −Max HP.

If an attack would reduce you below −Max HP, stop at −Max HP instead.

## Out of Combat

After combat ends, surviving Protagonists heal to full HP.

This clears Fading. It does not remove Deep Wounds.

# 7. Movement, Range, and Terrain

## Movement Pool

Movement pool is:

> Movement = 6 + Body + Speed bonuses.

Movement may be split freely before, between, or after actions.

Movement does not cost Beats.

There is no Dash action.

The Conductor should treat movement as permission for stylish action: wall-runs, slides, swings, vaults, rail grinds.

The bracket summary is:

| Body Score | Movement |
|---:|---:|
| 0–2 | 6–8 |
| 3–5 | 9–11 |
| 6–8 | 12–14 |
| 9–11 | 15–17 |
| 12 | 18 |

## Jumping

Jumping does not cost Beats.
You may freely jump over any gap less or equal to your jump distance. If the jump exceeds that distance, you may be asked to roll for it by the Conductor or find a different way to traverse.

| Body Score | Jump Distance |
|---:|---:|
| 0–2 | 3 tiles |
| 3–5 | 3 tiles |
| 6–8 | 6 tiles |
| 9–11 | 9 tiles |
| 12 | 9 tiles |

Graceful doubles jump distance.

A Body 12 character with Graceful jumps 18 tiles.

## Range Bands
All ranges include your tile as well and all tiles around you up to the max tiles.

| Range | Distance |
|---|---|
| Melee | Adjacent tiles only |
| Short | Up to 3 tiles |
| Medium | Up to 6 tiles |
| Long | Up to 9 tiles |
| Very Long | Up to 12 tiles |

## Terrain and Hidden

There is no universal cover mechanic.

Terrain does not grant defense bonuses unless an ability, hazard, or fiction explicitly creates an obstacle.

An obstacle may provide:

- full obstruction, which blocks line of sight;
- partial obstruction, which grants +5 DV as an exception.

Solid Manifestations and Barrier effects can create obstacles.

Difficult terrain costs 2 Movement per tile.

## Hidden

A Hidden Being cannot be directly targeted by attacks requiring line of sight.

To become Hidden:

- you must be out of line of sight or heavily obscured;
- spend 1 Beat;
- make a Grit clash against the highest Body of any enemy that could observe you.

If no enemy is actively observing, the Conductor may call for a Medium Grit check instead.

Hidden ends when the Being:

- makes an attack;
- casts a Manifestation;
- becomes clearly visible;
- otherwise reveals themselves.

First attack from Hidden:

> +5 AV.

## Forced Movement

Pushed or pulled Beings move in a straight line.

If pushed into an obstacle:

- stop before the obstacle;
- take 5 Entropy damage.

If pushed into another enemy:

- both take 5 Entropy damage.

Against mooks, this damage deals 1 Harm.

## Fall Damage

Falls greater than 6 tiles deal 5 Melee damage per additional 3 tiles, or fraction thereof.

Reduce fall damage by:

> 5 × Physical Tier.

| Body Score | Physical Tier | Fall Reduction |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0–5 | 1 | 5 |
| 6–8 | 2 | 10 |
| 9–12 | 3 | 15 |

Graceful doubles this reduction.

Fall damage counts as an Attack Value and can be defended if the fiction allows.

# 8. Equipment, Inventory, and Upgrades

## Loadout and Inventory

Each Protagonist has:

- 1 equipped Melee weapon;
- 1 equipped Ranged weapon;
- 3 Inventory Slots.

Switching between equipped weapons is Free.

Items and weapons occupy 1 slot.

Heavy objects or people occupy 2 slots.

Drawing from Inventory costs 1 Beat.

There is no ammo tracking. Reloading is Free.

There are no hand restrictions.

# 9. Combat Round and Beats

## Round Structure

Each round has three steps:

1. Player Phase — All Protagonists act using the Spotlight.
2. Enemy Phase — All enemies act using the Spotlight, controlled by the Conductor.
3. Cleanup — Lingering round effects end, timers advance, the Conductor updates objectives and hazards.

## Spotlight

Within a phase, there is no fixed turn order.

Whoever has the Spotlight may spend their own Beats, move, use abilities, and hand the Spotlight to another participant who still has Beats.

The first Protagonist with the Spotlight is the one with the highest Mind. If tied, the players choose who starts. The starting Protagonist should be known before combat begins.

Handing the Spotlight does not refresh anything. All once-per-round limits belong to the individual character.

## Ambush

Normally, the Player Phase comes first.

If the Conductor declares an ambush:

- Round 1 begins with an Enemy Phase.
- Surprised Protagonists cannot use Swift or Cadence abilities during that Enemy Phase.
- The Player Phase then begins normally.

## Beats and Action Costs

Each Protagonist has 2 Beats per round. Unused Beats are lost.

| Cost | Action |
|---:|---|
| 2 Beats | Chain, Greater Manifestation, powerful Technique, complex action |
| 1 Beat | Basic attack, Lesser Manifestation, use item, draw weapon, stabilize ally, Hide |
| Free | Switch equipped weapon, reload, open door, speak a short sentence |

## Commitment

You cannot attack the same target more than once per round unless you Chain.

This applies per character, per round, regardless of Spotlight handoffs.

AoE attacks count as attacking every Being they directly affect.

Splash damage on other targets does not commit those targets.

## Tag

Some abilities have the Tag keyword.

Tag abilities activate during Spotlight handoffs.

Tagging always requires the receiving player’s agreement.

There is no universal reward for handing off the Spotlight — the payoff comes from Tag abilities.

# 10. Attacks and Chaining

## Attack Value

Attack dice are printed on the weapon or Manifestation.

Attributes do not add attack dice unless an ability explicitly says so.

For standard weapons, use the Weapon Attack table.

The listed weapon attack line already includes the weapon’s Quality bonus.

Updated AV formula:

> AV = Weapon Attack Total + printed ability bonuses + CF if Chaining.

For Manifestation attacks, use the Manifestation baseline table in Section 14.

No Attribute Modifier is added.

## Open Information

When a player attacks:

> The Conductor states the enemy’s defense value before the roll.

When an enemy attacks:

> The Conductor states the attack value before the player chooses defense.

## Tie Rule in Attacks

If an attack result exactly equals a defense result:

- if the defender is a Protagonist, the defender wins;
- if the attacker is a Protagonist and the defender is not, the attacker wins;
- if both are Protagonists, the defender wins;
- if neither is a Protagonist, the defender wins.

When an attacker wins only because of the tie rule:

- against Mitigate, the attack deals 1 damage before Resistance;
- against Dodge, the attack is a glancing blow;
- against a static DV, the attack hits normally.

Against mooks:

- a normal hit deals 1 Harm;
- a critical hit deals 2 Harm.

## Chaining

A Chain is a 2-Beat combined attack against one target.

To Chain:

1. Spend both Beats.
2. Choose a primary attack.
3. Choose a different secondary weapon or Manifestation.
4. Roll the primary attack normally.
5. Add the secondary’s CF.
6. Resolve the final AV.

Final AV:

> Final AV = Primary Roll + Primary Bonus + Secondary CF + modifiers.

You cannot Chain a weapon to itself or a Manifestation to itself.

You may Chain:

- weapon + weapon;
- weapon + Manifestation;
- Manifestation + different Manifestation;
- Manifestation + weapon.

Both attacks must be usable at the target’s range.

## Melee Momentum

If the follow-up attack is Melee:

> You may move up to 3 tiles for free between the primary and follow-up attack.

This movement must end with the target in Melee Range.

## Secondary Effects

When a Manifestation is used as the secondary CF source:

- add its CF;
- add its damage type;
- add its simple effect if the final attack connects.

Do not add AoE, zones, healing, automatic triggers, or complex utility unless the ability explicitly says otherwise.

If an Exhaust Manifestation is used in any part of a Chain, it becomes exhausted.

## Chaining Factor

CF is precalculated.

> CF = floor(Maximum Possible Value ÷ 2) − Penalty.

| Maximum Value | Penalty |
|---:|---:|
| 1–19 | 1 |
| 20–29 | 2 |
| 30–39 | 3 |
| 40–49 | 4 |

Continue the pattern for higher values.

Maximum Possible Value means the maximum non-exploding total of the weapon or Manifestation dice plus permanent bonuses, such as weapon quality and printed Manifestation bonuses.

Exclude:

- critical explosions;
- CF itself;
- situational bonuses;
- temporary effects.

Printed CF overrides the formula.

## CF Examples

| Pool | Max | CF |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1d6 | 6 | 2 |
| 1d8 | 8 | 3 |
| 1d10 | 10 | 4 |
| 2d6 | 12 | 5 |
| 2d8 | 16 | 7 |
| 1d8 + 5 | 13 | 5 |
| 2d6 + 5 | 17 | 7 |
| 3d6 | 18 | 8 |
| 3d8 | 24 | 10 |
| 3d10 | 30 | 12 |

# 11. Defense

Each combatant has two defenses:

- Mitigate
- Dodge

Each combatant normally has 1 Mitigate and 1 Dodge per round.

These refresh at the start of their side’s phase.

Bosses refresh defenses once per round at the start of the round.

## Choosing Defense

When an attack is declared:

1. The Conductor states AV.
2. The defender chooses:
   - Take It;
   - Mitigate;
   - Dodge;
   - Burn, if both defenses are spent.

## Take It

Take full AV as damage.

This does not spend a defense.

## Defense Values by Score and Armor

MDV is keyed by Body score and Armor Bonus.

DDV is keyed by Mind score and Armor Bonus.

Averages are provided for calibration. In play, roll the listed dice.

| 0–2 | Refined | 2d4+5, avg 10 | 2d6+5, avg 12 |
| 0–2 | Masterpiece | 2d4+10, avg 15 | 2d6+10, avg 17 |
| 3–5 | Basic | 2d6+0, avg 7 | 2d8+0, avg 9 |
| 3–5 | Refined | 2d6+5, avg 12 | 2d8+5, avg 14 |
| 3–5 | Masterpiece | 2d6+10, avg 17 | 2d8+10, avg 19 |
| 6–8 | Basic | 2d8+0, avg 9 | 2d10+0, avg 11 |
| 6–8 | Refined | 2d8+5, avg 14 | 2d10+5, avg 16 |

Dodge beats Mitigate at every row by construction.

## Mitigate Outcomes

Compare AV and MDV using the attack tie rule.

If the defender wins, damage is 0.

If the attacker wins:

> Damage = AV − MDV.

Minimum damage is 0.

If the attacker wins only because of a tie, damage is 1 before Resistance.

## Dodge Outcomes

| Outcome | Condition | Damage |
|---|---|---:|
| Total Success | Defender wins resolution | 0 |
| Glancing Blow | Attacker wins by tie, or AV exceeds DDV by 1–5 | Half AV rounded down; minimum 1 if attacker won by tie |
| Full Hit | AV exceeds DDV by more than 5 | Full AV |

## All-In Defense

### All-In Dodge

Sacrifice your Mitigate.

Effect:

> +5 DDV for this attack.

Both defenses are spent afterward.

### All-In Mitigate

Sacrifice your Dodge.

Effect:

> +5 MDV for this attack.

Both defenses are spent afterward.

## Guard-Break

If a combatant has spent both Mitigate and Dodge, they are Guard-Broken.

While Guard-Broken:

- further attacks land automatically;
- no defense roll is made;
- damage is full AV.

Burn may still be used as an emergency defense.

## Defensive Burn

Burn may be used only after both Mitigate and Dodge are spent.

When you Burn:

- gain 1 Burn Token;
- immediately use Mitigate or Dodge against one attack.

Maximum 2 Burns per round.

There is no Offensive Burn.

### Penalty

At the start of your next turn, lose 1 Beat per Burn Token.

Burn Tokens are removed at the end of your next turn.

# 12. Damage and Critical Hits

## Damage Types

| Type | Rule |
|---|---|
| Melee | Standard physical damage |
| Ranged | Standard projectile damage |
| Arcane | Standard Manifestation damage |
| Entropy | Cannot be Mitigated or Dodged; Weak/Resistant/Immune still apply |
| Aeon | True damage; ignores defense, resistance, immunity, and reduction |

## Weakness, Resistance, and Immunity

| Interaction | Effect |
|---|---|
| Weak | Attack gains +5 AV |
| Resistant | Reduce final damage by 5 |
| Immune | No damage and no related status effect |

Aeon ignores all of these.

## Multi-Type Attacks

If an attack contains multiple damage types:

- Weakness and Resistance cancel if both apply.
- Immunity to the primary damage type negates the attack.
- Immunity to only a secondary CF type counts as Resistance.

## Damage Calculation Order

1. Calculate base AV.
2. Apply Weakness.
3. Resolve defense.
4. Apply Resistance.
5. Apply Immunity if relevant.
6. Apply final damage.

Minimum damage is 0 unless the damage is Aeon.

## Critical Hits

Combat rolls use bounded critical hits.

If any die shows its natural maximum:

> Roll one additional die of the same size and add the new result.

If multiple dice show their maximum:

> Roll one additional die for each.

No die can explode more than once.

This applies to:

- attack rolls;
- Dodge rolls;
- Mitigate rolls;
- Manifestation attack rolls.

Skill checks use the normal Hits explosion system instead.

## Critical Hits Against Mooks

A critical hit against a mook deals 2 Harm instead of 1.

# 13. Status Effects

Status Effects deal Entropy damage.

Entropy damage:

- cannot be Mitigated or Dodged;
- can be reduced by Resistance;
- is prevented by Immunity.

## Stacking Rule

A Being can have one stack of each Status Effect.

If a second stack is applied:

> The effect PROCs and is removed.

## Removal Rule

Un-proc’d Status Effects are removed at the end of the afflicted Being’s turn.

A Being’s turn is its Spotlight activation within a phase.

## Burst

Fire, ice, explosion, or volatile energy.

### Standard

At the end of the afflicted Being’s turn, take 5 Entropy damage.

### PROC

When Burst is applied a second time:

- target takes 10 Entropy damage;
- all other Beings in Short Range take 5 Entropy damage;
- Burst is removed.

## Drain

Bleed, poison, siphoning, or spiritual damage.

### Standard

Whenever the afflicted Being makes an Offensive Action or spends Movement, it takes 5 Entropy damage.

If it does neither, it takes no Drain damage.

### PROC

When Drain is applied a second time:

- target takes 10 Entropy damage;
- the attacker that proc’ed it heals 5 HP with Overflow;
- Drain is removed.

## Twist

Dizziness, confusion, sensory distortion.

### Standard

Until the end of the afflicted Being’s turn, −5 to all Defense Values.

### PROC

When Twist is applied a second time:

- the target’s next Attack Value is −10;
- the attacker that proc’ed it gains +5 Movement immediately;
- Twist is removed.

## Status Effects Against Mooks

When a Status Effect or PROC would deal Entropy damage to a mook:

- normal Status damage deals 1 Harm;
- PROC damage deals 2 Harm to the primary target;
- secondary AoE PROC damage deals 1 Harm.

# 14. Manifestations

Manifestations are supernatural abilities powered by Soul. They are weaponized memories.

Requirement:

> You must have Soul 1+ to use Manifestations.

## Manifestation Types and Exhaust

| Type | Cost |
|---|---:|
| Lesser Manifestation | 1 Beat |
| Greater Manifestation | 2 Beats |

Strong Manifestations have Exhaust.

Manifestations with Exhaust can be used only once per scene.

Exhaust is the main limiter for:

- Tier V–VI Manifestations;
- huge AoE;
- delayed detonations;
- battlefield control;
- extreme utility;
- powerful Augments.

## Apotheosis

If you have the Apotheosis skill:

> Permanently reduce a known Manifestation’s Beat cost by 1, minimum 1.

Apotheosis may be taken only once per Manifestation.

Because ability math is keyed to final Beat cost, if Apotheosis or another effect changes a Manifestation’s Beat cost, use the baseline row matching the final Beat cost and Exhaust status.

## Known Manifestations

| Soul | Gain |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Unlock Lesser Manifestations. Learn 1 Lesser Manifestation. |
| 3 | Learn 1 additional Lesser Manifestation. |
| 5 | Learn 1 additional Lesser Manifestation. |
| 7 | Unlock Greater Manifestations. Learn 1 Manifestation, which may be Lesser or Greater. |
| 9 | Learn 1 additional Manifestation. |
| 11 | Learn 1 additional Manifestation. |

The Memory Enhancement adds additional Lesser Manifestations.

## Manifestation Dice and Bonuses

Manifestations use two real baseline types: Lesser and Greater.

There is no Quality axis. Manifestations are learned, not bought.

For Archetype Techniques and other combat abilities, use the same baseline logic: key the math to Beat cost, not label.

If a Manifestation does not list dice, use the following baseline:

| Final Beat Cost | Exhaust? | Baseline Dice | Avg | CF |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 Beat | No | 1d6 | 3.5 | 2 |
| 2 Beats | No | 2d6 | 7 | 5 |
| 1 Beat | Yes | 1d8 | 4.5 | 3 |
| 2 Beats | Yes | 2d8 | 9 | 7 |

An Exhaust version bumps the baseline by one die-size step.

Never add a flat number just because the ability has Exhaust.

Printed Manifestation bonuses, usually +5 or +10, are per-ability author discretion. They are layered on top of the baseline and are not a systemic tier.

If a Manifestation has a printed bonus, add it to the baseline dice and recalculate CF unless a printed CF is given.

Printed dice and printed CF override the baseline.

Manifestations do not add Soul Modifier.

## Manifestation Range

All Manifestations are Short range unless an Augment or printed rule says otherwise.

The Reach Augment increases Manifestation range by one step.

If you have the Arcane Reach skill:

> Your Manifestations increase their range by one step without counting as an Augment.

## Manifestation Entries

A Manifestation entry should include:

- Name
- Type: Lesser/Greater
- Cost
- Dice
- CF
- Damage Type
- Keywords
- Effect
- Augment notes
- flavor line

Default values unless stated otherwise:

- Range: Short
- Damage Type: Arcane
- effects apply if the Manifestation connects
- persistent effects last 1 round maximum
- CF is precalculated

# 15. Augments

Augments are free. They represent shaping a Manifestation into a new form or behavior.

## Augment Limit

By default:

> You may apply one Augment per Manifestation casting.

With the Specialization skill:

> You may apply up to two Augments.

If any Augment used has Exhaust, the entire Manifestation casting becomes exhausted.

## Reach

Cost: Free.

Increase the Manifestation’s range by one step:

> Short → Medium → Long → Very Long.

Strong version:

> Increase range by two steps. This version has Exhaust.

## Solid

Cost: Free.

The Manifestation becomes a tangible construct.

Choose Melee or Ranged damage type instead of Arcane.

Can create a physical object, barrier, or construct lasting until end of your next turn.

Solid constructs are obstacles:

- partial, +5 DV;
- full, blocks line of sight.

Can be destroyed by 10+ damage.

Strong version:

> If used to create a delayed trap or explosive rune, has Exhaust.

## Fluid

Cost: Free.

Leaves liquid memory behind.

Create a hazard puddle in Short Range lasting until end of your next turn.

A Being entering or starting its turn there takes 5 Entropy damage.

The puddle is difficult terrain.

Strong version:

> If applying control, push, bind, or repeated status, has Exhaust.

## Gas

Cost: Free.

Becomes a lingering cloud in Short Range lasting until end of your next turn.

Attacks through, into, or out of the cloud suffer −5 AV.

Beings ending their turn in the cloud take 5 Entropy damage.

Strong version:

> If cloud lasts 2 rounds, fills a large area, or applies a status effect, has Exhaust.

## Plasma

Cost: Free.

Volatile conductive energy.

If the Manifestation deals damage, apply Burst to the target.

If the target is standing in a Fluid or Gas hazard:

- the hazard detonates;
- target takes +5 Entropy damage;
- all other Beings in Short Range of the hazard take 5 Entropy damage;
- the hazard is removed.

Strong version:

> Plasma detonation has Exhaust.

## Trigger

Cost: Free.

Strong version:

> Exhaust.

The Manifestation is set dormant and activates when a simple, perceivable, specific condition is met.

Only one triggered Manifestation may be active at a time.

Duration:

> Until end of scene or triggered.

Effect cannot exceed original power budget.

If damaging an unaware mook, may auto-hit.

Bosses and alert enemies should get defense.

# 16. Manifestation Effects

Manifestations should use a small set of effect tags.

Status effects, zones, push/pull, and bind values are intentionally flat across the level range. Do not bracket-scale them unless deliberately altering that design pattern.

## Effect Tags

| Effect | Description |
|---|---|
| Damage | Makes an attack roll using printed dice and CF. Damage is resolved normally. |
| Status | Applies Burst, Drain, or Twist. Applies only if the Manifestation connects and deals at least 1 damage unless stated otherwise. |
| Push / Pull | Lesser: 3 tiles. Greater: 6 tiles. If target collides with a wall or another Being: stop, take 5 Entropy damage. |
| Bind | Reduce target’s Movement to 0 until end of its next turn. Stronger bind effects should have Exhaust. Bosses should usually have Tenacity or immunity. |
| Zone | Short radius, lasts until end of your next turn, affects Beings when they enter or start turn there, once per Being per round, damage usually 5 Entropy. Zones applying control or status should usually have Exhaust. |
| Barrier | Partial obstacle (+5 DV) or full obstacle (blocks line of sight). Duration 1–2 rounds. Can be destroyed by 10+ damage. |
| Heal | Lesser: 5 HP. Greater: 10 HP. Exhaust: 15 HP or Overflow. Subject to H.T. unless Overflow. |
| Utility | Sensing, revealing, moving objects, creating light, opening paths, altering terrain. If contested: Soul or Derived check. Should not replace core skills unless highly specialized or Exhaust. |

# 17. Manifestation Resolution

## Attack Manifestations

1. Declare target or point.
2. Apply Augments.
3. Spend Beats.
4. Conductor states defense value.
5. Roll Manifestation AV.
6. Resolve damage, status, and riders.

## Non-Damaging Manifestations

Roll its attack value.

Defender chooses Dodge or Mitigate.

If the attacker wins, the effect applies.

If the defender wins, the effect fails.

Use the general tie rule.

If the effect is harmless and the target is willing, no roll is needed.

## Zones

Zones usually do not require an initial attack roll.

They affect Beings when they enter the zone or start their turn there.

If a zone applies damage, it is usually Entropy.

Entropy zone damage cannot be Mitigated or Dodged.

## Automatic Triggers

Delayed effects:

- cost no Beats;
- require no action;
- happen when their condition is met.

Delayed attacks:

- roll when triggered;
- allow defense as normal;
- use your Manifestation AV at that moment.

A Manifestation should have only one automatic trigger.

# 18. Archetypes and Abilities

## Archetype Structure

Each Archetype has:

- one combat passive;
- one narrative passive;
- four signature combat abilities.

At Levels 1, 3, and 6, choose one signature ability.

One of the four abilities is permanently left unchosen.

If a Protagonist begins play above Level 1, they receive all archetype choices granted by lower Levels.

## Signature Abilities

Signature abilities are Techniques.

They must use the existing action economy and keywords.

Archetype abilities should not:

- add Attribute Modifiers;
- create permanent buffs;
- ignore commitment freely;
- add unlimited status effects;
- create extra attacks outside Chaining.

Strong abilities should use Exhaust.

## Signature Ability Math

When designing, converting, or evaluating a signature combat ability, use the ability attack baseline from Section 14.

| Final Beat Cost | Exhaust? | Baseline Dice | CF |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 Beat | No | 1d6 | 2 |
| 2 Beats | No | 2d6 | 5 |
| 1 Beat | Yes | 1d8 | 3 |
| 2 Beats | Yes | 2d8 | 7 |

Key the math to final Beat cost, not the ability’s label.

## Left-Behind Ability

The unchosen fourth ability remains locked.

The Conductor may allow it to be learned later through a major Realize condition and MP cost.

# 19. Enemies

There are three broad enemy categories:

- Mooks
- Elites
- Bosses

## Enemy Design Bands

Enemy numbers are derived from the PC combat math.

| Band | Attribute Bracket | Mook DV | Mook AV | Elite DV | Elite AV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Early | 3–5 | 8 | 9–10 | 12–13 | 13–14 |
| Mid | 6–8 | 14 | 15–16 | 18 | 19–20 |
| Late | 9–11 | 16–17 | 18 | 21–22 | 23 |
| End | 12 | 21–23 | 24–25 | 27–28 | 29–30 |

Mook DV tracks PC weapon average for the Band.

Mook AV tracks PC DDV.

Elite runs +4–5 over Mook on both DV and AV.

## Mooks

Mooks do not track HP. They track Harm.

Normal hit = 1 Harm.

Critical hit = 2 Harm.

When a mook’s Harm track is filled, it is defeated.

| Harm | Role |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Fodder |
| 3 | Standard mook |
| 4 | Tough mook or elite minion |

Mooks use static Defense Values and static Attack Values from the Enemy Design Bands table.

Use the Band’s Mook DV and Mook AV as the baseline for mooks.

Differentiate Fodder, Standard, Tough, and elite minion roles primarily through Harm, fiction, positioning, and behavior rather than by adding flat DV/AV bonuses.

If an elite minion should function as a true elite combatant, use the Elite row instead.

Use the general tie rule.

Mooks usually act once during the Enemy Phase.

The Conductor may group identical mooks for speed.

## Elites

Elites are stronger than mooks but not full bosses.

Elites use the Band’s Elite DV and Elite AV.

Elite endurance is not formula-locked. The Conductor may use:

- a Harm track of 5–6;
- or a scene-appropriate HP pool.

Elites usually have 2 Beats during the Enemy Phase.

They may have:

- 1 Mitigate and 1 Dodge;
- or a static DV from the Elite row, depending on presentation and speed of play.

## Bosses

Bosses use full HP pools and an Influence Value.

Boss DV/AV has no final formula in this pass.

Use the relevant Band’s Elite values as a reference point, then adjust according to fiction, boss role, and desired pressure.

Boss HP and Influence Value are GM-set.

## Influence Value

The Influence Value is the number of Beats the players can collectively spend before the boss interrupts.

When players spend Beats equal to the boss’s Influence Value:

1. The boss immediately takes its own turn.
2. Reset the Influence counter.

The boss also acts normally in the Enemy Phase.

| Influence | Feel |
|---:|---|
| 2 | Extremely aggressive boss |
| 3 | Standard boss |
| 4 | Slow, monumental boss |

## Boss Defenses

Bosses refresh defenses once per round at the start of the round.

Unless a special rule says otherwise:

- bosses have 1 Mitigate and 1 Dodge per round;
- bosses do not Burn;
- bosses can be Guard-Broken.

## Boss Tenacity

Because status effects can be strong, bosses should often have Tenacity.

Examples:

- immune to one status type;
- reduce status Entropy damage by 5;
- ignore non-Exhaust movement reduction;
- once per round, shrug off one status effect.

# 20. Conductor Rules

## State Values Openly

Always state:

- enemy defense values;
- enemy attack values;
- relevant range bands;
- obvious hazards.

Do not make players guess numbers.

## Weaknesses and Resistances

Weaknesses and resistances are not automatically known.

Reveal them when:

- fiction makes them obvious;
- an ability reveals them;
- a player succeeds on an appropriate check.

## Objectives

Every combat should have at least one of:

- an objective;
- a clock;
- an escape route;
- reinforcement pressure;
- an environmental hazard.

Combat should not only be “kill all enemies.”

## Movement as Permission

If a player describes a stylish way to cross distance, allow it if the tile cost is reasonable.

Tiles are a guideline for positioning, not a cage.

## Grouping Enemies

For speed, the Conductor may:

- roll one attack for a group of similar mooks;
- use static DVs;
- apply Harm in batches.

## Content Band Guidance

When building enemies, weapons, Techniques, or loot:

1. Pick the relevant content Band.
2. Read the matching Attribute Bracket across the defense, weapon, Manifestation, and enemy tables.
3. Prefer die-size steps when increasing combat power.
4. Keep status effects, zones, push/pull, and bind values flat unless intentionally altering that design assumption.

# 21. Handler and Memory Law

Every Protagonist has a Handler — AR contact lenses, an earpiece, and a fingerprint console.

The Handler links citizens to the city grid.

## Handler Functions

The Handler provides:

- identity verification;
- city grid access;
- communication;
- commerce;
- AR overlays;
- navigation;
- public data access.

## Grid Signal

| Signal | Effect |
|---|---|
| Strong | Full Handler functions available. |
| Weak | AR and communication work, but commerce, scanning, and remote services may fail. |
| None | Handler functions are offline except local cached data. |
| SafeMode | AR bonuses are disabled, but mental protection is improved. |

Roots, Fracture zones, Rain, and corporate dead zones may reduce signal.

## AR Assist

Once per scene, while Grid Signal is Strong or Weak, a Protagonist may use AR Assist to gain +1d6 to:

- navigation;
- identification;
- research;
- commerce;
- scanning;
- social presentation through AR styling.

In combat, AR Assist costs 1 Beat and may be used to scan an enemy.

On a successful Mind check, the Conductor reveals one of:

- weakness;
- resistance;
- hazard;
- faction affiliation;
- obvious tactical trait.

## SafeMode

During Scheduled Downpour, Fracture static, AR attacks, or memory distortion events, the Handler enters SafeMode.

While in SafeMode:

- AR Assist is disabled;
- remote commerce is disabled;
- non-local communication is impaired;
- public grid services may be unavailable.

SafeMode also grants +1d6 to resist:

- hallucination;
- memory distortion;
- AR spoofing;
- sensory manipulation.

## Memory Sanctity

The Handler cannot access, store, transmit, or modify personal memories.

This is both law and technical design.

To extract, view, alter, or steal memories, a Being needs:

- physical contact or a direct neural link;
- illegal black-market hardware;
- a Manifestation capable of memory intrusion;
- or a forbidden Fracture-device.

Memory crimes carry severe consequences.

Open memory crimes may make factions Hostile and raise Infamy.

## Handler Security

Handler conflicts are skill checks.

Use:

- Mind for hacking, tracing, spoofing, and system analysis;
- Grit for subtle manual manipulation, physical taps, and black-market rigging;
- Will for resisting social engineering, AR intimidation, and memory intrusion.

Failure can result in:

- location traced;
- identity flagged;
- Infamy gain;
- false AR overlay;
- locked commerce;
- corrupted communication;
- faction attention.

## Handler Commerce

Legal purchases can be made remotely through the Handler if Grid Signal is Strong.

Illegal purchases require:

- a black-market contact;
- untraceable Kaph;
- Roots access;
- or a Safe Zone during Nyctophilia hours.

Openly purchasing illegal goods through the Handler may raise Infamy or make a faction Hostile.

## Handler Mods

Handler mods use the upgrade system and require the Handler Mods Aptitude.

| Mod | Effect |
|---|---|
| AR Combat Overlay | Once per scene, gain +1d6 to a combat scan or tactical check. |
| SafeMode Reinforcement | SafeMode also protects one ally within Short Range. |
| Encrypted Burst | Once per gig, reduce Infamy gained from digital traces by 1. |
| Offline Cache | Retain maps, records, and translation data in low-signal zones. |
| Black-Market Router | Buy illegal goods remotely, but gain Infamy if traced. |
| Memory Shield | Gain +1d6 to resist memory intrusion and forced recollection. |
| Ghost Profile | Once per session, reroll a check to avoid identification. |

# 22. Hazards: Noesis Rain and Fractures

## Noesis Rain

Noesis Rain falls several times each month when the city dome opens.

Rain has a Rating from 1 to 3.

| Rain Rating | Check Difficulty |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Easy |
| 2 | Medium |
| 3 | Hard |

When exposed to Noesis Rain at the start or end of a round, make a Body or Will check.

On failure:

- take 5 Entropy damage;
- gain Twist.

On a critical failure:

> Suffer a brief hallucination or memory distortion as well.

Protected buildings, Rain Lounges, and sealed transport do not require Rain checks.

SafeMode grants its bonus to resist Rain effects.

During Noesis Rain, reduce Infamy gained from a gig by 1.

## Fractures

Fractures are reality-bending zones connected to alternate timelines or dimensions.

Fractures have a Rating from 1 to 3.

| Fracture Rating | Effect |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Minor distortion |
| 2 | Reality instability |
| 3 | Severe breach |

When interacting with a Fracture, make a Soul or Will check.

Difficulty equals the Fracture Rating.

On failure:

- take 5 Aeon damage;
- or gain Twist.

On success:

> Gain a clue, shortcut, memory fragment, or unstable loot.

Severe Fractures may deal 10 Aeon damage on failure.

Aeon damage ignores defense, resistance, immunity, and reduction.

# 24. Factions, Infamy, and NPCs

## Faction Standing

Faction Standing is tracked per crew, not per Protagonist.

Each faction has one of three states:

| Standing | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Allied | The faction supports the crew. |
| Neutral | The faction has no strong opinion. |
| Hostile | The faction opposes the crew. |

## Allied

Allied factions may provide:

- safehouse access;
- free basic gear;
- emergency extraction;
- unique high-reward gigs;
- faction-specific intel.

A faction usually becomes Allied after repeated meaningful help or a major favor.

## Neutral

Neutral factions offer standard gigs and standard prices.

Most factions begin Neutral.

## Hostile

Hostile factions:

- refuse service;
- may report the crew;
- may send enforcers;
- may post bounties;
- do not offer legitimate gigs.

If a Hostile faction appears to offer a gig, it is usually a trap.

A faction usually becomes Hostile after direct harm, theft, betrayal, or public crimes against it.

Killing a faction boss makes that faction Hostile immediately.

Rival factions may improve by one step when a faction boss is killed.

## Moving Standing

| Event | Change |
|---|---|
| Complete a gig for the faction | Move one step toward Allied |
| Harm the faction’s enemy | Move one step toward Allied |
| Harm the faction directly | Move one step toward Hostile |
| Kill the faction’s boss | Become Hostile immediately |
| Clean gig with no attribution | No change |

Once a faction is Hostile, it cannot be placated through ordinary favors.

Only a major story event can restore a Hostile faction to Neutral.

## Infamy

Infamy measures how loudly the city knows the crew.

Infamy is tracked per crew.

Range: 0–3.

| Infamy | State | Effect |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Cold | No special attention. |
| 1 | Warm | +25% prices. Authorities are suspicious. |
| 2 | Hot | +50% prices. Hunters appear. Bloom shops become wary. |
| 3 | Burning | +50% prices. Named hunters or corporate teams arrive. All factions treat the crew as one step worse. |

At Infamy 3:

- Allied factions may act like Neutral;
- Neutral factions may act like Hostile;
- already Hostile factions remain Hostile.

## Gaining Infamy

After a gig, the crew gains +1 Infamy if any of the following happened:

- civilians saw violence or Manifestations;
- bodies were left behind;
- witnesses escaped;
- heavy property damage occurred;
- a boss or named NPC was killed;
- the gig took place in the same district as the previous one.

Maximum +1 Infamy per gig.

During Noesis Rain, reduce Infamy gained from that gig by 1.

## Reducing Infamy

| Method | Effect |
|---|---|
| Complete any gig | −1 |
| Lay low for one downtime day | −1 |
| Pay bribes | −1 per 300 Kaph |
| Free gig for an Allied faction | −1, and the faction vouches for you |
| Full wipe to 0 | 500 Kaph × current Infamy, requires a Handler data-scrubber |

## NPCs and Leverage

NPC relationships are per Protagonist.

The Conductor tracks NPC relationships.

Each significant NPC is either Friendly, Neutral, or Hostile.

## NPC Tags

Every significant NPC has three tags for the Conductor.

Players discover them through roleplay, investigation, intel gathering, or social checks.

| Tag | What It Is |
|---|---|
| Want | What the NPC desires. |
| Fear | What the NPC is afraid of. |
| Secret | Something the NPC hides. |

## Leverage

If you know an NPC’s Want, Fear, or Secret, you have leverage.

When you use leverage in a social scene:

> +1 automatic Hit on the relevant check.

If you deliver the Want, credibly threaten the Fear, or expose the Secret:

> +2 automatic Hits instead.

Using Fear or Secret damages the relationship afterward.

## Social Clash

When persuasion is contested, use a Derived check.

Convince uses Will opposed by Will.

Command uses Aura opposed by Aura.

Resolve social clashes in 1–3 rolls.

Use the general tie rule.

Leverage, Infamy intimidation, and NPC Standing apply as relevant.

## Burning Bridges

A Protagonist can burn a relationship with a Friendly or Neutral NPC to guarantee success on one social action related to that NPC.

No roll is made. It simply works.

Afterward, the NPC becomes permanently Hostile.

This cannot be undone.

## Favors

NPCs can owe you favors, and you can owe them.

A favor is a one-time promise.

Earning a favor:

> Do something significant for the NPC.

Calling in a favor:

> No roll required — the NPC delivers.

Refusing to honor a favor you owe makes that NPC Hostile.

## Defense

Each combatant has 1 Mitigate and 1 Dodge per round.

Mitigate:

> Damage = AV − MDV.

Dodge:

- Total Success = 0 damage.
- Glancing Blow, tie or AV exceeds by 1–5 = half AV rounded down.
- Full Hit, AV exceeds by more than 5 = full AV.

Dodge beats Mitigate at every bracket by construction.

# 28. Designer’s Guide

This section provides the design logic behind the updated numbers.

## B1. Content Band → Attribute Bracket

Pick a Band, then read every relevant table at the matching bracket.

This gives a pre-balanced set of numbers.

| Band | Attribute Bracket | Quality | Weapon Tier |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Early | 3–5 | Basic | I |
| Mid | 6–8 | Refined | II |
| Late | 9–11 | Refined | III |
| End | 12 | Masterpiece | III |

The 0–2 bracket is included in the tables for completeness, but it is dump-stat territory for enemy and loot calibration. Skip it unless deliberately building weak or introductory content.

## B2. Enemy Design Bands

Enemy numbers are derived from the PC combat math, not hardcoded independently.

| Band | Mook DV | Mook AV | Elite DV | Elite AV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Early | 8 | 9–10 | 12–13 | 13–14 |
| Mid | 14 | 15–16 | 18 | 19–20 |
| Late | 16–17 | 18 | 21–22 | 23 |
| End | 21–23 | 24–25 | 27–28 | 29–30 |

Mook DV tracks PC weapon average for the Band.

Mook AV tracks PC DDV.

Elite runs +4–5 over Mook on both DV and AV.

Bosses do not yet have a full formula. Use Elite as a reference point, then adjust HP, DV/AV, and Influence based on the desired boss feel.

## B3. How to Design Something New

Use the following process:

1. Pick the Band.
2. Read across every relevant table at that Band’s Attribute Bracket.
3. The numbers already balance against each other.

### Weapons and Items

Pull dice straight from the Weapon Attack table at the intended Band and Quality.

If nothing fits, the item belongs in a different Band.

### Techniques, Abilities, and Manifestations

Use the Manifestation/Ability baseline table.

Key the math to Beat cost, not flavor text.

A 1-Beat ability is a Lesser slot no matter what it is called.

A 2-Beat ability is a Greater slot no matter what it is called.

| Final Beat Cost | Exhaust? | Baseline Dice | CF |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 Beat | No | 1d6 | 2 |
| 2 Beats | No | 2d6 | 5 |
| 1 Beat | Yes | 1d8 | 3 |
| 2 Beats | Yes | 2d8 | 7 |

### Enemies

Start from the Band’s Mook row.

Reskin through Harm:

- Fodder: 2 Harm
- Standard mook: 3 Harm
- Tough mook: 4 Harm
- Elite minion: 4 Harm, or use Elite numbers if it is a true elite

Do not add flat DV/AV just to make a mook special unless you are deliberately moving it into Elite space.

### Exhaust Content

Anything with Exhaust bumps one die-size step.

Never add a flat number only because the ability is Exhaust.

Examples:

- Lesser 1d6 becomes Lesser Exhaust 1d8.
- Greater 2d6 becomes Greater Exhaust 2d8.

### Status Effects, Zones, Push/Pull, and Bind

These remain intentionally flat across the whole level range.

Leave them alone unless you are deliberately breaking that pattern too.

## Open Design Notes

Two issues remain intentionally unresolved in this pass.

### Boss DV/AV Formula

Bosses currently have no fully systematized DV/AV formula.

The working rule is:

> Use the relevant Band’s Elite values as a reference, then adjust by fiction and desired boss pressure.

This is functional, but worth systematizing later if you want Bosses fully consistent with the rest of the math rather than GM-eyeballed.

### Ranged Identity

Ranged is mechanically the safer-but-weaker option by design. It sits one die-size step behind Melee at every tier.

However, Ranged does not yet have an active hook comparable to Melee Momentum.

This remains an open question:

- Is safer-but-weaker enough identity for Ranged?
- Or does Ranged need its own active payoff mechanic?

No rule in this pass changes that status.
